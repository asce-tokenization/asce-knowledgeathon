Frequently asked questions

Clear answers to help you choose a direction, form a team, and prepare your submission.

17 questions

Who can participate?

Undergraduate and graduate students, professionals, faculty, researchers, practitioners, and public-sector participants are welcome. Final eligibility details will be confirmed before competition launch.

Do I need to be an ASCE member?

Each team must include at least one ASCE member. The current guidance does not require every team member to belong to ASCE.

Can I participate on my own?

Yes. The current draft allows teams of one or more people, with no formal maximum. A solo participant must meet the team ASCE-membership requirement.

What if I do not have a team?

Indicate that you are looking for teammates during registration. Team-formation support is planned; the committee will announce the confirmed process. The resources page includes a worksheet to help describe your interests and skills.

What does tokenization mean in this challenge?

It means representing an engineering asset as a discrete, reusable, verifiable knowledge unit with context intact. Useful elements can include provenance, metadata, assumptions, validity limits, attribution, and integrity checks. Cryptocurrency or a production blockchain is not required.

Do we have to build software?

No. A concrete, reviewable artifact may be a schema, workflow, knowledge unit, governance design, notebook, software prototype, or another demonstrated mechanism. You must show how it works in practice.

Do all teams use the same dataset?

No. Select, reuse, or construct an engineering asset that fits your civil or environmental engineering problem. You must have the necessary rights and explain source permissions and reuse restrictions.

Are the three pathways separate tracks?

No. Select one primary pathway to clarify your emphasis. Pathways are not mutually exclusive, and every submission must address the core engineering knowledge challenge.

Can we use AI tools?

Yes. Disclose the tools, what they were used for, the parts of your work they affected, and any risks or dependencies. Explain how your team reviewed and validated AI-assisted work. Your team remains responsible for the submission.

What do we submit?

A concise project brief, concrete artifact or prototype, trust and reuse explanation, ecosystem growth statement, AI-use disclosure, licensing / ownership note, and a practical demonstration video of no more than 10 minutes. The submission guide explains link sharing.

Can we submit GitHub, YouTube, or Google Drive links?

Yes. Submit project materials through accessible GitHub repository, YouTube, or Google Drive links. A repository or Drive folder can organize the written material and artifact; a YouTube or Drive video link can provide the demonstration. Reviewers must be able to open every required item.

Does a slide presentation count as the demonstration?

Slides can introduce the context, but the required video must show the artifact, prototype, schema, workflow, notebook, or governance process in actual use. Slides alone are insufficient.

How long should the project brief be?

Keep it concise and complete. Final page-length guidance will be announced by the committee. The editable project brief template provides the expected sections.

What are the dates and deadlines?

Dates are not yet confirmed. The timeline page lists the planned sequence of milestones. Final dates and time zones will be published there and on the updates page.

How much are the awards?

The proposed award pool is $6,000: one $1,200 winner in each pathway, plus either two $400 honorable mentions or one $800 honorable mention / nomination per pathway. Final arrangements will be confirmed before launch.

Will registration details or project links be made public?

Registration data is intended for event administration. Participant profiles and gallery entries should be published only with team permission and committee review. Share only project materials you are authorized to share.

How do I contact the organizing committee?

Email asce.tokenization@gmail.com with questions about the scope, eligibility, team formation, submissions, or schedule. Common clarifications may be shared with all teams.

Still have a question?

The organizing committee can help with challenge scope, team formation, eligibility, and submission expectations.
