Engineering Knowledge-a-Thon

Rules & evaluation

Understand the challenge, prepare the right evidence, and make your work easy to review.

The core challenge

Transform engineering assets into verifiable, reusable knowledge products that can grow across communities and support trustworthy human and AI use.

Choose a civil or environmental engineering domain and one primary pathway. The pathways are not mutually exclusive; your submission may combine ideas from more than one.

Current guidance: This page summarizes the draft participant specification. Final rules, eligibility details, dates, and award arrangements will be confirmed by the organizing committee.

Participation & team rules

  • ParticipantsUndergraduate and graduate students, professionals, faculty, researchers, practitioners, and public-sector participants are welcome.
  • TeamsMinimum one member; no formal maximum in the draft guidance. At least one member must be an ASCE member.
  • LeadershipDesignate a team lead. One person may lead only one team. Participation in additional non-lead roles is subject to final committee guidance.
  • Assets and methodsExisting or newly created resources may be used with appropriate rights. No common dataset, prescribed model, or blockchain deployment is required.

Required submission components

  1. Project brief

    A concise explanation of the problem, domain, assets, approach, trustworthiness challenge, and broader ecosystem relevance. Final page-length guidance will be announced.

  2. Core artifact or prototype

    At least one reviewable knowledge unit, schema, workflow, diagram, notebook, software prototype, or demonstrated governance framework.

  3. Trust and reuse explanation

    Explain provenance, integrity checks, applicability, limitations, human review, and what is needed for responsible reuse.

  4. Ecosystem growth statement

    Show how your approach connects to at least one broader ASCE community or ecosystem function.

  5. Practical demonstration video

    A required video of no more than 10 minutes showing the artifact in use. Brief presentation context is welcome; slides alone do not meet the requirement.

  6. AI disclosure and licensing / ownership note

    Describe AI assistance and validation. Identify ownership, source permissions, contributor attribution, and restrictions on reuse.

Submit accessible GitHub repository, YouTube video, or Google Drive links through the submission form. Include a clear entry point and review instructions.

AI use, attribution & responsibility

AI tools may support ideation, research, coding, data processing, writing, visualization, testing, and prototype development. Disclose the tools used, their purpose, the affected parts of the work, and any limitations or dependencies.

Your team remains responsible for accuracy, originality, safety, rights, and ethical use. Review and validate AI-assisted outputs. Do not place confidential, sensitive, or restricted data in public AI tools without appropriate permission.

Clearly state what you own, what you reuse, and the terms under which your submission may be shared. Detailed licensing and ownership provisions will be finalized by the committee.

What reviewers will look for

Use these nine criteria to guide your preparation. Scoring weights and the evaluator roster have not yet been announced.

CriterionEvidence to make visible
FocusA defined engineering knowledge ecosystem problem.
Trustworthiness DesignProvenance, integrity, interpretability, applicability limits, reviewability, and governance.
Technical ClarityAn artifact, schema, workflow, or governance design that reviewers can understand and inspect.
Prototype / Artifact DemonstrationA practical demonstration of the artifact in use, beyond a slide presentation.
Ecosystem RelevanceA connection beyond a single case, dataset, or project.
Long-Term ValueStewardship, incentives, governance, cybersecurity, and sustainability.
Domain GroundingA clear connection to a real civil or environmental engineering context.
AI Use Disclosure and ValidationSpecific disclosure and evidence that AI-assisted work was reviewed and validated.
Communication QualityClear explanations for both domain experts and reviewers from other backgrounds.

Reviewers should be independent from competing teams and recuse themselves when a direct or perceived conflict exists. Sponsor support or institutional affiliation should not influence scoring. Final review procedures will be announced.

Proposed awards: $6,000

The following recognition is proposed for each of the three challenge pathways:

  • Tokenization and Verification AwardPathway Award Winner: $1,200 × 1 for the strongest overall submission aligned with the pathway.
    Honorable Mention / Nomination: $400 × 2 or $800 × 1 for promising work with strong potential, creativity, or ecosystem relevance.
  • Community Onboarding and Ecosystem Growth AwardPathway Award Winner: $1,200 × 1 for the strongest overall submission aligned with the pathway.
    Honorable Mention / Nomination: $400 × 2 or $800 × 1 for promising work with strong potential, creativity, or ecosystem relevance.
  • Sustainability, Governance, and Stewardship AwardPathway Award Winner: $1,200 × 1 for the strongest overall submission aligned with the pathway.
    Honorable Mention / Nomination: $400 × 2 or $800 × 1 for promising work with strong potential, creativity, or ecosystem relevance.
Each pathway has a proposed $2,000 allocation. The committee will confirm the honorable-mention arrangement and final award terms before launch.
